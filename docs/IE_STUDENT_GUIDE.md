# Planning an IE exchange without a subscription

Sources reviewed on September 28, 2026. This independent project focuses on undergraduate attendance planning. Master's programs have separate policies. Check current course instructions and Program Management decisions before relying on an estimate.

## How strict is the policy?

The [2026–27 bachelor policy](https://docs.ie.edu/documents/Attendance_Policy_Bachelor_Programs.pdf) expects full attendance. Below 80% means 0.0 in both examination calls. Personal travel counts toward the absence limit; joining an in-person class remotely does not erase an absence. Late arrival or early departure also counts. Async attendance depends on assigned work.

Specified documented reasons can qualify for exclusion. Submit evidence through Virtual Help Desk within seven days; Program Management decides. A professor's informal agreement is insufficient. Read the linked policy for qualifying categories and exceptional cases.

## What does exchange status change?

IE's [2026–27 exchange factsheet, hosted by McGill](https://www.mcgill.ca/mcgillabroad/files/mcgillabroad/ay_26-27_ieu_undergraduate_explorer_student_program_exchange_partnership_fact_sheet.pdf) explicitly applies attendance rules to local and exchange students and describes strict enforcement by academic offices. GEO cannot override them. Orientation is mandatory and attendance starts on day one.

Timetables can vary weekly. Course availability and home-university approvals constrain enrollment; selecting courses does not guarantee a long weekend. Finals are in person, and June resits are not guaranteed for exchange students.

There is a source discrepancy: the factsheet's enrollment section refers to reaching the 20% limit, whereas the bachelor policy specifies falling below 80%. Its treatment of interviews is also less specific. Ask Program Management to resolve your case; this app cannot settle that discrepancy.

My reading: these are substantial academic constraints on travel, not evidence of a blanket travel ban. The documents describe policy and institutional enforcement claims; they do not measure how every professor enforces it in practice.

## Use the planner before booking

1. Verify each course's full session count. Count numbered sessions, not calendar blocks. At the default 80%, 15 units produce a mathematical allowance of three; a two-unit block consumes two.
2. Record what actually happened. Missing records stay unknown. Only mark an absence **Excused** after approval, and explicitly select **Do not count** in the course policy if appropriate. Pending requests should continue counting.
3. Enter the full away interval, including transfers. Compare an earlier departure with leaving after class. Check exams, presentations, and group commitments independently of the remaining allowance.
4. Review async deadlines and record confirmed attendance in the asynchronous class editor. Completing your checklist does not confirm the professor's record.
5. Keep room for unexpected events and export a backup. A number above zero is a calculation from your inputs, not permission to travel.

## Why free?

The project exists to make this personal planning workflow available without a recurring fee. Its code and features are available to everyone under MIT. It does not promise a hosted service or automatic synchronization.

For context, [SkipFreely](https://skipfreely.com)'s public Terms & Conditions, inspected September 28, 2026, list EUR 19.99 for 150 days of planner access, not a monthly charge. This is a dated pricing observation, not a checkout verification or a comparison of tested capabilities. Skip Budget's free license stands on its own.

Free source code alone does not remove setup friction: this edition still requires a local Node.js installation. Easier distribution is the next accessibility gap to validate with students. No competitor price or feature-parity claim is needed to justify making this project free.
